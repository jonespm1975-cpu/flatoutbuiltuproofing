Understanding roof falls and tapered boards

Work through original examples and a short self-check. This is a teaching resource, not a roof design tool.

Safety and design boundary: The calculator below illustrates simple geometry only. It does not establish compliant design falls, minimum finished falls, roof structure, thermal performance, drainage capacity, outlet sizes, loading, fixing patterns, fire performance or board schedules. A competent designer and system manufacturer must approve all project-specific specifications.

1. What a fall ratio means

A nominal slope of 1 in N means one unit of vertical change for every N units of horizontal run. In a straight, constant-slope example:

Height change (mm) = horizontal run (metres) × 1,000 ÷ ratio denominator.

For a purely illustrative 6 m run at 1:80: 6 × 1,000 ÷ 80 = 75 mm height difference. This does not mean 1:80 is suitable for a particular roof; design falls and achieved finished falls are different considerations.

3. Why real layouts need more than one slope

Many roofs have multiple outlets, changes of direction, crickets, sumps, valleys, existing deck deflections and upstands. Tapered boards must form a coordinated scheme, not simply a uniform wedge across the whole roof.

High points

Review the approved layout for ridge positions, thresholds and available build-up height.

Low points and outlets

Check designed water paths and local outlet details, including any sumps and overflow provision.

Board schedule

Match board IDs, thicknesses and orientation to the manufacturer's project-specific plan.

Site checks

Record substrate condition, level variations, deviations from the layout and required designer decisions before covering work.
