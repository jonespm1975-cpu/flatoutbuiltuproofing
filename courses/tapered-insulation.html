Tapered Insulation

1. Drainage design

Identify outlets, falls, valleys, crickets, roof deflection and potential ponding. Confirm design falls with the competent designer rather than guessing from roof appearance.

2. Reading tapered-board plans

Check board references, thickness transitions, orientation arrows, sumps and perimeter upstands. Escalate missing or contradictory layout details.

3. Thermal and moisture strategy

Check minimum insulation thickness, thermal performance, condensation assessment, air and vapour control, and deck compatibility.

4. Substrate and fixing

Check deck condition, dryness, structural suitability, wind-uplift design and approved attachment method. Keep insulation protected from weather.

5. Felt roof interface

Use the manufacturer's approved cover board, membrane system, fire classification and detailing. Never torch directly onto unapproved insulation.

6. Quality control

Record board layout, thicknesses, joints, drainage transitions, outlet access and deviations. Photograph before covering and retain as-built records.

Knowledge check

  1. Why can design falls differ from finished falls?
  2. Who approves substitutions of tapered boards?
  3. What evidence is needed at handover?

Answer guidance: deflection and tolerances; the competent designer/system manufacturer; as-built layout, product records and inspection evidence.
