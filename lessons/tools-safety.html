Flat Out Built-Up Roofing

Roofing Tools and Safety

Unpublished educational draft. Review by a competent UK roofing trainer before publication.

Free beginner guide. Only trained and authorised personnel should carry out hazardous roofing work.

1. Working at height

Plan access, edge protection, fragile-surface controls, weather limits and emergency rescue. PPE alone is not an adequate substitute for collective fall protection.

2. Measuring and marking

Use tapes, straightedges and chalk lines on a stable work surface. Check drawings and drainage geometry; keep away from unprotected edges.

3. Knives and cutters

Inspect blade and guard condition. Use a suitable cutting surface, control the cutting line, and dispose of blades in approved sharps containers.

4. Rollers and hand tools

Inspect handles and working faces. Reduce repetitive strain and maintain clear walkways.

5. Powered tools

Follow manuals, guards, dust/noise controls, electrical safety and vibration exposure limits. Remove damaged equipment from service.

6. Gas torch awareness

Gas equipment creates serious burn and fire risks. Only competent authorised operatives should use it, under appropriate hot-work controls, fire watch and emergency arrangements. This beginner guide does not teach ignition or torch application.

7. Close-out

Secure tools and cylinders, clear waste, inspect for smouldering materials, and record defects or near misses.
