New to Flat Roofing?

Start here with essential awareness training for roofing labourers and beginners. These guides are free to read and do not require payment or an account.

Important safety notice: Reading these guides is not a qualification, competency assessment, or substitute for supervised practical training. Work at height, power tools and torch-on roofing must follow the site risk assessment, employer procedures, equipment instructions and relevant UK safety requirements.

⚡ 110V Corded Tools

Awareness for site transformers, leads, circular saws, drills, grinders and other corded equipment.

  • Use only tools you have been trained and authorised to operate.
  • Before use, check the tool, plug, cable, guards, blade or disc and any site inspection label or record required by your employer.
  • Follow the site electrical arrangements for 110V tools, transformers, cable routing and weather exposure; 110V equipment still presents risks.
  • Isolate equipment before changing accessories or carrying out checks permitted by the manufacturer.
  • Stop, disconnect safely and report damaged cables, missing guards, overheating, unusual vibration or other faults. Do not attempt unauthorised repairs.

▣ Battery-Operated Tools

Awareness for cordless impact drivers, drills, reciprocating saws, circular saws and other battery tools.

  • Inspect the tool, battery, charger, guards and accessories before use.
  • Use the correct manufacturer-approved battery, charger and accessories.
  • Keep batteries away from excessive heat, damage and moisture; do not use swollen, damaged or overheating batteries.
  • Prevent accidental starting and remove the battery before changing accessories where the instructions require it.
  • Store and charge batteries in the approved area; report damage and never improvise battery repairs.

♜ Harness & Working-at-Height Safety

Fall prevention comes first. A harness does not make an unprotected roof safe.

  • Follow the work-at-height hierarchy: avoid height where possible, then prevent falls with suitable collective protection before relying on personal systems.
  • Harnesses, lanyards and anchors must be selected and used under a competent person's plan.
  • Check equipment condition and inspection records as required; never use suspect or damaged equipment.
  • Correct fit, anchor suitability, clearance, edge risks and rescue arrangements require competent practical instruction.
  • Do not begin harness-dependent work without a suitable rescue plan and supervision.

✚ Free Health & Safety

Basic expectations for new labourers on a flat roofing site.

  • Attend the site induction and understand the task briefing, safe access routes and emergency arrangements.
  • Wear the PPE specified by the risk assessment, such as suitable footwear, hard hat, eye, hand, hearing and respiratory protection.
  • Respect edge protection, openings, fragile surfaces and exclusion zones.
  • Keep work areas tidy; use safe manual handling methods and appropriate lifting aids.
  • Report near misses, hazards, unsafe conditions and injuries immediately. Stop and seek guidance if unsure.

♨ Hot Works & Torch-On Roofing

Fire prevention awareness only — not instructions for operating a roofing torch.

  • Only competent, authorised workers should carry out torch-on work under the site-specific method statement and permit arrangements where required.
  • Identify combustible construction, concealed voids, flammable materials and nearby people before hot works are authorised.
  • Ensure suitable extinguishing equipment, fire watch and post-work checks are in place according to the site plan.
  • Check cylinder, hose and regulator condition through the approved inspection process; never use damaged equipment.
  • Know how to raise the alarm, evacuate and contact emergency services. Never improvise hot-work procedures.

⚒ Roof & Equipment Maintenance

Simple awareness of inspections, defects and planned upkeep.

  • Roof inspections must use safe access and suitable edge protection; do not walk onto an unassessed roof.
  • Look for visible issues such as blocked outlets, ponding, damaged flashings or membrane defects, and report them.
  • Do not undertake repairs, hot works or electrical maintenance without the required competence and authorisation.
  • Keep tool inspections and servicing in line with manufacturer guidance and employer procedures.
  • Record and escalate defects so a competent person can decide the appropriate action.

For further guidance, refer to the UK Health and Safety Executive, your employer's safety arrangements and the relevant manufacturers' instructions. This educational content does not certify a worker as trained or competent.
