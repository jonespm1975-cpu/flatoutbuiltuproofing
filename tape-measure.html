Using and reading a tape measure

Before any roofing training, learn to measure accurately, understand metric and imperial graduations, and record dimensions consistently.

Learning time: about 15–20 minutes, including practice and assessment.

Take the knowledge check

1. Know your tape

  • Case and lock: the case holds the blade; use the lock to hold a reading if appropriate.
  • Hook and zero: the hook aligns the zero reference to an edge; a small designed movement accommodates the hook thickness. Replace a damaged or unreliable tape.
  • Blade and markings: use the correctly labelled metric or imperial scale, not a mixture of the two.

Practise on a clear, stable bench at ground level. Retract the blade slowly under control; keep fingers clear of the sharp edges and retracting hook. Never use a measuring exercise as authorisation to access a roof or work near hazards.

2. Read metric markings

One millimetre (mm) is the smallest division on a typical metric tape. Ten millimetres make one centimetre (cm). One hundred centimetres, or one thousand millimetres, make one metre (m). Most UK roofing drawings specify dimensions in millimetres; always confirm the stated units.

Worked example: the arrow is seven small divisions beyond 120 mm, so the reading is 127 mm, or 12.7 cm.

3. Read imperial markings

Some tapes also show feet and inches. One foot contains twelve inches. On a typical inch scale the longest marks identify whole inches; shorter marks indicate halves, quarters, eighths and sixteenths. Check how your own tape is graduated.

Worked example: six sixteenths after two inches is two and three eighths inches (2⅜ in). One inch equals exactly 25.4 mm. Do not round a specified measurement unless the required tolerance permits it.

4. Measure, record and check

  1. Confirm the drawing units and the correct start and end reference points.
  2. Inspect the tape and align the zero reference to the first edge.
  3. Keep the blade straight and supported along the measured path.
  4. Read square-on, record the number and units, and repeat the measurement before marking.
  5. If using a non-zero starting mark, subtract it from the end reading. For example, 735 mm minus 100 mm equals 635 mm.

For inside measurements use a tape designed for this purpose or the manufacturer's stated case-length method; do not guess around a bend.

5. Apply the same skill to all four roofing areas

Built-Up Roofing

Practise recording the length and width of a sample board and checking a measured span against a drawing.

Tile & Slate Roofing

Practise measuring sample tiles or slates and recording dimensions in millimetres; consult the approved layout for real work.

Metal & Sheet Roofing

Practise measuring a bench sample of sheet material, checking both reference edges and recording orientation.

Liquid Applied Roofing Systems

Practise measuring the sides of a rectangular sample area and recording width and length separately.

6. Hands-on practice at ground level

Use a tape and three safe objects on a flat bench. Measure each twice, write each result in millimetres, convert one to centimetres and metres, and ask a supervisor or another learner to compare the readings. Try an offset measurement, then subtract the start mark. Repeat any inconsistent result.

Checking your own answers is useful, but this lesson and quiz are not a practical qualification or competency sign-off.
