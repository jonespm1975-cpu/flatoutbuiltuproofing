Learning outcomes

What you will learn

By the end of the lesson, learners should be able to describe key pre-start checks and explain why the approved roof design and compatible manufacturer system must guide the work.

  • Record roof use, existing coverings, outlets, upstands, rooflights and signs that need further investigation.
  • Recognise deck checks for OSB3 and plywood, including condition, support, fixings, moisture and structural suitability.
  • Explain how falls and drainage routes affect outlets, gutters and areas vulnerable to ponding.
  • Identify the specified vapour control layer, insulation, underlays, cap sheet, primers and detailing products as one compatible system.
  • Recognise hot-work fire hazards, roof-edge and fragile-surface risks, and the need for competent planning.
  • Plan inspection hold points and records for concealed and completed work.
Lesson outline

Seven parts

  1. Start with the roof

    Consider the roof's use, structure, existing coverings, outlets, upstands, rooflights and visible moisture concerns.

  2. Check the deck

    Review OSB3 or plywood condition, support spacing, thickness, fixings, moisture and the project design.

  3. Map falls and drainage

    Trace the planned water path through outlets and gutters; note level changes and ponding risks.

  4. Confirm system compatibility

    Check the specified vapour control, insulation, underlays, cap sheet, primers and detailing products.

  5. Plan safety controls

    Recognise hot-work and work-at-height hazards, safe access needs and competent supervision.

  6. Set a quality plan

    Agree inspection hold points for the deck, falls, insulation, laps, junctions and finished waterproofing.

  7. Knowledge check

    Review short questions about compatible systems, drainage and high-risk penetrations.

Safety and design limits

Roofing torches, hot bitumen, powered tools, roof edges and fragile rooflights can cause serious injury, fire or death. Practical work requires competent people, appropriate training, safe access, a site-specific risk assessment and method statement, fire precautions and the manufacturer's instructions.

OSB3 and plywood are examples only. This lesson does not specify board thickness, fixing patterns, falls, upstand heights or membrane details for a particular roof. Confirm the project design with the responsible designer and system manufacturer.

Online training is not a substitute for practical supervision, site-specific planning or a recognised qualification.
